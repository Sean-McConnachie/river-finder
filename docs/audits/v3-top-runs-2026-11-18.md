# Audit: v3's top unlisted runs (2026-11-18)

First desk audit of what the v3 model (`models/v3.json`, LightGBM `gbm_terrain` recipe) puts at the
top nationally. The list is `models/audits/v3_top_runs.csv`, built with:

```bash
uv run river-finder ml audit --model v3 --top 60
```

## How the list is made

- **Unlisted reaches** are reaches draining 2 km² or more that no known section covers (guide
  research plus `labels/known_runs.toml`).
- A trained model puts its own training rivers at the very top. 5,768 of the 5,948 reaches in v3's
  national top 1% are on guide sections. So the cut is taken among unlisted reaches only: the top 1%
  of unlisted reaches by v3 score.
- Hot reaches are chained down the main stem into **runs** (one cooler reach is allowed inside a
  run), at least 1 km long. That gives 415 runs. The CSV keeps the best run of each of the 60
  top-ranked rivers. Unnamed reaches count as one river per run.
- **`oof_pct`** is the same percentile from the out-of-fold CV scores of `gbm_terrain`, averaged
  over 5 repeats. Those scores come from models that never saw the reach's catchment. It checks that
  a run is not an in-sample or single-fit artefact. The top tail is noisy even between CV repeats:
  two repeats share only 62–66% of their top-1% reaches.
- Road distance is in the CSV for the reader. Access never changes the order.

This is a desk audit. I used the model's numbers, the coordinates and the river names, plus what I
know of NZ rivers. I did not look at imagery or walk the rivers. Anything marked "check" needs a
person with the guidebooks, aerial imagery or local knowledge.

**1. Most of the top is extensions of known runs.** 43 of the 60 rivers already have a guide section.
The candidate is the stretch above, below or between the listed sections. Examples:

- Rangitikei, Rangitaiki, Kawarau, Mohaka, Ngaruroro and Waipunga;
- Whanganui, Tongariro, Buller, Landsborough and Taipo.

Some of these are probably runnable and simply not written up as separate sections. Others are the
flat water between sections of a multi-day trip. For finding *new* rivers, filter these out: the
viewer has "Only runs not in the guides", and the CSV has `river_in_guides`.

**2. The model rewards size, so flat big-river reaches rank high.** 11 runs are under 3 m/km. All are
large rivers in confined valleys with mean annual floods of 150 to 2,300 m³/s:

- Aorere, Mohaka, Maruia and Motu;
- the Tasman and Bay of Plenty Wairoa rivers;
- Karangarua and Haast;
- two unnamed South Westland valley-floor reaches.

The guides include grade 2 multi-day rivers, so v3 has learnt that "a big river in a gorge" looks
like a guide river. These are low-value leads for whitewater. Follow-up: a gradient floor or a
style filter for the discovery ranking, or ranking candidates by their style × size percentile.

**3. DEM flats.** Two runs have exactly 0 m/km over more than a kilometre:

- the **Roaring Lion River** (Kahurangi, −41.20, 172.43);
- an unnamed reach of the lower **Arawhata** valley (−44.05, 168.73).

The 8 m DEM is flat there. The Roaring Lion is a steep gorge river in reality, so its rank comes
from flood size and gorge depth, not from a measured gradient. Its real gradient needs a LiDAR check.

**4. Hydro-affected reaches.** Flood statistics are natural (REC1). They overstate the flow in
diverted reaches:

- **Rank 31** (unnamed, Hawke's Bay, −38.80, 177.12, 112 m/km on a 352 km² catchment) is at the
  outlet of Lake Waikaremoana. The lake drains through its landslide dam and the hydro scheme, so
  the steep reach is the dam face and not a run. The same check applies to similar spots.
- **Waipori** (rank 63) is below the Mahinerangi hydro dam.
- The lower **Wairoa** in Bay of Plenty (rank 67, flat) is below the Kaimai hydro scheme.

Follow-up: a hydro-diversion mask, or a flag on these reaches.

Most of the strongest leads are remote South Westland and Fiordland rivers. That fits the
project's premise that the unexplored terrain is where access is hard. **Check** them against the
guidebooks first: a few, such as the Mahitahi and the Mikonui, may be documented in sources the guide
research did not cover.

**6. Data issues found along the way**

- The **Waiapu** candidate (rank 85, East Cape) has a mean annual flood of 646 m³/s from 79 km².
  That is 8 m³/s per km², which is plausible on the West Coast but not on the East Cape. The REC1
  flood join probably took a larger reach's value. **Check** it.
- The guides spell two rivers differently from REC2: "Manganui-a-te-ao" vs "Manganui-o-te-ao", and
  "Routeburn River" vs "Route Burn". Their sections are placed by coordinates, so the labels are
  right. The audit's `river_in_guides` flag now also uses the REC2 names of reaches under guide
  sections.
- One Manganui-a-te-ao section ("put-in + length") runs 24 reaches up **Orautoha Stream**, a side
  branch, instead of staying on the main river. Follow-up.
- Five runs fall under the 95th percentile out-of-fold: Makuri, Koranga, the Waikaremoana outlet,
  Ohinemuri and Arahura. They are less robust than the rest.
